EX09: Data Analysis for Continuous Improvement

Project Summary

The integrated analysis of the course survey data, processed through custom columnar and concat utilities, provides a data-driven foundation that supports the implementation of specialized conceptual walkthrough videos. By utilizing convert_columns_to_int to transform qualitative survey responses into a quantitative format, the resulting relational analysis reveals a clear positive correlation between perceived course difficulty and weekly time commitment.

Consequently, the data suggests that students are already maximizing their "time on task" and would benefit more from targeted conceptual frameworks than from simply increasing their study hours. By addressing the specific needs of this non-major population through structured visual aids, the course can better facilitate proficiency without exacerbating the high time demands already documented in the workload distribution.

Visualizations & Findings

1. Major Distribution

Finding: The categorical distribution of the data confirms a high density of students from diverse majors beyond computer science. This indicates that a large portion of the enrollment may lack the technical mental models required for rapid mastery, supporting the need for non-major focused interventions.

2. Weekly Study Hours

Finding: The workload histogram indicates a significant cluster of students spending over ten hours per week on coursework. This suggests that students who find the material most challenging are reaching a point of diminishing returns in terms of manual effort.

3. Difficulty vs. Time Spent

Finding: There is a clear positive correlation between perceived difficulty and weekly hours. This refutes the idea that students struggle due to lack of effort; instead, it proves that the most challenged students are often those working the hardest.

Final Conclusion

The combined analysis of the anonymized survey data from both instructors’ sections reveals that students are not struggling due to a lack of effort, but rather due to the complexity of the material itself. Implementing specialized support initiatives, such as technical walk-through videos, could create substantial value by providing targeted conceptual frameworks rather than just encouraging more "time on task."

However, adopting this initiative involves significant trade-offs, such as the high "cost of production" for instructors and the risk that providing too much starter code could inadvertently create a "crutch," potentially reducing the development of independent debugging skills.

Future refinements of this project should include longitudinal tracking of student interest levels and more granular data collection to identify exactly which units, such as loops or memory management, trigger the highest spikes in difficulty. Additionally, future work should include a comparative A/B test to measure if these videos lead to a measurable increase in "Understanding" scores without increasing the overall time burden on the student.
