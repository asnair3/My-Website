Final Conclusion

The combined analysis of the anonymized survey data from both instructors’ sections, processed using custom data utilities like concat and convert_columns_to_int, reveals a clear positive correlation between perceived course difficulty and the total hours students invest each week. By using convert_columns_to_int to process survey responses, the resulting relational scatter plot reveals a clear positive correlation between perceived difficulty and weekly time commitment, suggesting that students are not struggling due to a lack of effort, but rather due to the complexity of the material itself. While the categorical distribution confirms that the course successfully reaches a diverse range of majors beyond Computer Science, the workload histogram indicates a significant cluster of students spending over ten hours per week on coursework. This suggests that students who find the material most challenging are already maximizing their personal effort and may be reaching a point of diminishing returns. Implementing specialized support initiatives, such as major-specific review sessions or technical walk-through videos, could create substantial value for these students by providing targeted conceptual frameworks rather than just encouraging more "time on task."

However, adopting this initiative involves significant trade-offs, such as the high "cost of production" for instructors and the potential for "information overload" if too many supplementary materials are added to an already rigorous workload. There is also a risk that providing too much starter code could inadvertently create a "crutch," potentially reducing the development of independent debugging skills which are essential for long-term success. To refine this idea in the future, the project should move toward longitudinal tracking to identify exactly which units, such as loops or memory management, trigger the highest spikes in difficulty.

Future refinements of this project should include longitudinal tracking of student interest levels and more granular data collection to identify exactly which tasks are consuming the most time, ensuring that future course interventions directly address the most significant conceptual hurdles. Future work should also include a comparative A/B test across different sections to measure if these videos actually lead to a measurable increase in "Understanding" scores, ensuring that instructional refinements are addressing the most significant conceptual hurdles without increasing the overall time burden on the student.
